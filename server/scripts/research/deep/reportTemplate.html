Insider Buying Deep Study

Do insider purchases, combined with the stock's chart, contain repeatable and realistically tradable patterns? Every SEC purchase filing in the project was tested against later price moves, using only information public at the time.

No insider-buying pattern was a robust, tradable edge for buying at the next regular-hours open.

Of registered tests, none survived the pre-committed train → validation → test protocol once the market regime was controlled for. The data does show one very robust effect: insider purchase filings move the stock, but almost all of that move happens before the next open. The only place it can be captured is in the minutes after an after-hours publication. That is the most valuable direction for further work, and it is still exploratory.

Executive summary

  • FactThe market prices insider purchases immediately. At the first open after publication, stocks gap up versus SPY by a median (positive in of events, t = ). The same stocks on ordinary days show . The median gap was positive in every one of 12 months and in all three test periods.
  • FactAfter the open, the edge is gone. Buying at that open did not beat SPY or other insider purchases in any validated pattern, at any horizon from 1 to 60 sessions.
  • ExploratorySpeed is the only lever found. For CEO/CFO personal open-market buys ≥ $250k published after the close, a buyer at the first after-hours trade (median min after acceptance) captured to the next open (t = ). That falls to at 25 bps per side and about zero at 50 bps.
  • WarningLate filings underperform. Purchases reported more than 4 days after the trade trailed other insider buys by / / (train / validation / test, 20 sessions). The test p-value of misses the pre-set 0.05 bar.
  • RejectedEvery pre-registered behavioural hypothesis failed. That includes clusters, CEO-after-others, escalating sizes, small-then-huge (RAPP-like), averaging down, buying after crashes, near lows, consolidation and breakouts. None was significant even in training after correction.
  • ContextThe whole universe of insider-bought stocks trailed SPY more over time (20 sessions: → → ). Judged only against SPY, any negative pattern looks "validated". This study therefore judges patterns against other insider purchases made in the same month.

Data inventory and quality

Method and pre-committed protocol

  • Event: one Form 4 filing with code-P purchases, evaluated at T = its SEC acceptance. Only filings accepted by T count. Duplicate reports are grouped point-in-time (a report filed later never changes an earlier view). Repeat purchases by one insider stay separate transactions but count as one buyer.
  • Features ( numeric + boolean): insider sequence (role order, repeat buys, size and price trends, buyers in window, price dispersion, buying surge, filing delay) and chart state from closes strictly before the entry session (returns, drawdown, distance from lows and highs, moving averages, volatility and contraction, volume spikes, gaps, relative strength, consolidation, breakout, move since the insider's trade, market trend).
  • Entry: . Exits: close after 1, 2, 3, 5, 10, 20, 40, 60 sessions. Costs: . Benchmarks: SPY (tradable view) and the month's average insider purchase (regime-controlled view).
  • Splits by publication date: train before , validation until , test after. An event counts in train or validation only if its exit falls before the next period starts (embargo).
  • Promotion: . All tests are corrected together (Benjamini–Hochberg). Standard errors are clustered by company, because several filings from one company aren't independent.
  • Discovery families: 30 pre-registered hypotheses, quintiles of every feature, tertile × tertile interactions of the 12 most informative features, a depth-3 regression tree and a ridge score (all fitted on train only).

Limitations and biases

  • Short history: about 11 months (), one market regime, with the backfill still incomplete ( of SEC business days). The test period is ~2 months; 40- and 60-session horizons are mostly pending there.
  • Not available: .
  • Survivorship: filings without usable prices are excluded (renamed, delisted or OTC symbols), which probably flatters results.
  • Daily bars for most entries; trade-level data only in the after-hours sample. Extended-hours spreads were not measured (no quotes method in the shared data client), so costs there are scenarios.
  • Exploratory after-hours design: it was chosen after seeing the full-data gap, so it needs fresh out-of-sample confirmation.
  • Data quality: extreme daily moves (>75%) across symbols; look like bad prints (reversed the next day). They're kept, and results use medians, winsorized means and clustered errors.

Recommendations

Most valuable next experiment

  1. A forward test of immediate after-hours entry on CEO/CFO personal open-market buys ≥ $250k: paper-trade from today with real detection latency and real bid/ask quotes, with the rule frozen now. It needs about 6 months and ~100 events to confirm or reject.
  2. Measure the platform's detection latency against SEC acceptance. The edge, if any, sits in the first 1–5 minutes.
  3. Extend history backwards (the running backfill) to test the late-filing warning and the opening gap across several regimes.

Possible Strategy Builder features (not implemented)

  1. Show the opening-gap context on every signal: the move from the last close to the first tradable price, so users see what was already priced in.
  2. A "late filing" flag (filed > 4 days after the trade) as a caution, and an offering-price flag.
  3. Extended-hours entry modelling with spread and liquidity, separate from regular-hours results.
  4. Always show the "all insider purchases this month" baseline and the out-of-sample split next to any strategy result.
