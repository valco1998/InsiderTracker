Insider Buyer Sequences

Does the order in which insiders buy within a month, combined with how much they invest and the chart, predict a gain of +10% or more, and how fast? Specialist agents rebuilt every monthly buying sequence point in time and compared it with matched stocks that had no insider buying. An independent auditor then tried to break the result.

No. A buyer's position in the month's sequence does not predict a tradable +10% gain, and neither does purchase size or chart shape.

About one in five insider buys touches +10% within 5 sessions, usually by the second session after entry. Comparable stocks without insider buying do the same. No position from #1 to #10+, and no "largest purchase", beat those controls in both the discovery and confirmation periods. All five frozen leads lost money in September–November. The information is real: counted from the last close before publication, insider buys reach +10% about 6 points more often than controls. But that move happens in the opening gap, before a public trader can get a price.

Other patterns, through the same +10% lens

  • FactUpside is not larger. Open-market buys touch +10% within 10 sessions less often than matched stocks (32.3% vs 35.9%). In confirmation, +10% within 5 sessions was 18.6% vs 24.5%.
  • FactDownside is smaller, partly. A −10% drop within 5 sessions happened 18.4% vs 23.1% of the time (t −4.5), most strongly after crashes. With better volatility matching, the confirmation effect shrinks to −1.3 points (not significant). It narrows the range; it is not a trade.
  • RejectedClusters, CEO/CFO, repeat buyers, rising or falling amounts, holdings %, buys after drops, consolidations, breakouts, support levels, volume, relative strength, volatility change, sales context, company history, first buy after a quiet period, liquidity tiers, publication time and purchase type: 54 variants and 349 tests. None raises the +10% rate after correction. Repeat, shrinking and breakout buys lose money.
  • FailedTwo discovery winners failed confirmation:
    • Buying while other insiders sold only through planned 10b5-1 sales: +1.93% → +0.50%.
    • A cluster near the 60-day low: +1.35% → −0.99%; equal to non-insider stocks with the same chart.
  • FactModels. A model for P(+10% within 5 sessions) works about equally well without insider inputs (AUC 0.789 vs 0.792). Volatility explains the hit rate.

Independent audit

  • No look-ahead. Point-in-time sequences, embargo (101,030 checks) and costs (22,353 recomputed) are all correct.
  • Identity rule. Switching to the app's identity rule changes 0 of 4,018 positions.
  • Found and corrected:
    • Coordinated board buys counted one trade several times, inflating two leads. The drawdown follower falls from +1.55% to +0.73%.
    • Part of the "less downside" effect is volatility mean-reversion.
    • A suspected partial-SEC-day bias is really a May-2026 effect.
  • Multiple testing. Of about 4,400 p-values across all three studies, the only positive executable deep-3 rule surviving correction was the low-60 cluster. It failed both later periods.
  • Verdicts:
    • sequence agent CONFIRMED, leads REFUTED;
    • targets agent CONFIRMED, "less downside" WEAKENED;
    • data-integrity agent CONFIRMED;
    • overall answer CONFIRMED: no.

Rejected hypotheses

Buyer #1, #2, #3, #4, #5, #6–7, #7, #8 and later. Largest purchase so far. Eventual largest purchase. CEO arriving late in a sequence. Escalating amounts. Fast followers. Followers after drawdowns. Rolling-30-day positions. All-purchase-type sequences, where offering- and fund-triggered positions were negative. Plus the 54 pattern variants above, the two discovery winners, and all deep-2 rules. Every test is listed in .cache/research/deep3/registry.jsonl: about 680 in deep 3 and 4,400 across all studies.

What would actually move this forward

  1. 1-minute bars for the 7,043 after-close and overnight filings (about 750–1,500 Alpaca requests, 5–10 minutes). These show how much of the +10% advantage is reachable between SEC acceptance and the open. That is the only place a real effect was found. Needs your approval.
  2. After-hours and premarket trades for all filings (about 6,800 requests, 45 minutes). Real bid/ask quotes need a new method in a shared production file, so separate approval.
  3. A daily-bar pack (77 requests, under a minute): exact split factors and 37 recovered events. A cleanup, not likely to change conclusions.
  4. Older SEC history (14–25 hours per year), the only independent test. Do this only after you confirm the stalled backfill process is dead. It is also the only way to test positions #4 and later with enough events.
  5. Optional zero-cost forward logging of new multi-buyer months: publication time, alert time, first trades. No trading.
