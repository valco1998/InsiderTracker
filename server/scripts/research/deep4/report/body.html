InsiderTracker · Deep study 4 · SEC Form 4 purchases, Dec 2025 – Oct 2026

Can a trading strategy be learned from how individual insiders buy?

We rebuilt every insider's buying history using only what was public at each filing, tested 23 frozen strategies built from buying style, track record, learned models and price confirmation, and had an independent auditor try to break the results.

No. Nothing learned from insider behaviour beat SPY after costs once tested on months it had not seen.

Insiders do have repeatable buying styles, and models can rank 30-day returns. But the ranking comes from ordinary price patterns that work just as well on stocks with no insider buying. Every frozen rule lost money in June–August and again in September. The apparent advantage over similar non-insider stocks turned out to be a side effect of how those comparison stocks were chosen. No strategy is worth paper trading.

How it was tested

Each purchase is entered at the official open of the first session after the SEC published the filing (plus 60 seconds), and held to the close 30 calendar days later. Costs are 5–100 bps per side by liquidity, and every result is net of costs and compared with SPY. Features use only filings accepted earlier and prices from before the entry day. An insider's track record counts only outcomes that had already finished.

  • FactThe chance of touching +10% is mostly volatility (AUC 0.71–0.76 from price features alone; insider features add nothing). The purchases most likely to touch +10% had the worst 30-day returns in June–August.
  • StableThe one consistent insider signal is negative: repeat buyers, 10b5-1 plan purchases and stocks that had already risen between trade and filing do worse, as studies 2 and 3 also found.

Why "insiders beat similar stocks" did not hold up

Insider purchases looked about 1 point better than matched non-insider stocks over 30 days. The auditor found the comparison stocks were chosen with hindsight: the price cache holds only companies where insiders bought at some point, and the rule "no insider purchase within 20 sessions" means every comparison stock had its own insider purchase coming 21 or more sessions later. Stocks drift down before insiders buy them, so the comparison group was dragged down.

DesignConfirmation

With comparison stocks chosen only from information available at the time, the difference was −1.8 points in design (t −2.5), +0.5 in confirmation and +1.0 in September, none of it a usable advantage. Measured from the last close before the filing, insider purchases are 2.6 points ahead in both periods: the news is priced in the opening jump, as study 2 found.

Insider buying styles

Ten observable styles were defined from price conditions at each purchase before any outcome was looked at. Of 2,360 insiders, 568 bought at least twice, and 87% of repeat buys were the same company within days. A style "repeats" if an insider's earlier purchase episodes predict the style of their next one, compared with shuffled labels within the same month.

Near-low, deep-drawdown, contrarian and following-other-insiders styles repeat (and did again in later periods). But at a different company bought at least 14 days later most of it vanishes, so the style mostly describes the stock rather than a habit the person carries. None of the styles picked purchases that beat SPY.

The answer

Can we learn a repeatable trading strategy from the historical purchasing behaviour of individual insiders? Not with this data. Three families of hypotheses failed, for different reasons:

  • Buying style (family A): styles repeat, but they describe the stock's state, and stocks in the same state without insiders behave the same.
  • Track record (family C): only 18.5% of design purchases had even one finished past outcome for that insider, and past results did not predict the next one (correlation 0.02).
  • Learned models and price checks (family B and models): the predictive part is generic price behaviour, and whatever was insider-specific in design reversed later.

The information in an insider purchase is real, but it shows up in the opening jump, before a public trader can buy.

Data that could change the conclusion

  1. Multi-year point-in-time Form 4 history (for example 2015–2025) with daily bars, so each insider has enough finished outcomes for a track record to be measurable. History here starts in December 2025.
  2. Daily bars for the whole listed market, so comparison stocks are not drawn only from companies insiders bought.
  3. Minute bars after the filing, to see whether any of the opening jump can be captured before the open.

None of these has been downloaded; each needs your approval.
