Insider Strategy Search

A second, deeper search for one tradable strategy in SEC Form 4 insider buying and price behaviour. Three specialists worked in parallel, the candidate rules were frozen with a hash, and an independent auditor tried to break the result.

No strategy survived. Nothing is worth real capital or a paper-trading slot yet.

The insider information is real but priced into the first trades after publication, before the next regular open, where a follower using daily data enters. After the open, insider stocks beat matched look-alike stocks by only about 0.2–0.3% over 0–1 sessions. The average round-trip cost is about 0.7%. The best candidate, CEO/CFO personal open-market buys of $250k or more held for 3 sessions, came from a handful of companies and lost money in the seen holdout.

Where the edge actually is: before the open

Extra opening gap of insider-bought stocks over matched look-alike stocks (same month, liquidity, prior 5/20-day move, drawdown, volatility), discovery period. Every group has t ≥ 3.8. A next-open follower pays this gap; it doesn't earn it.

  • Bigger position increases gap more: top vs bottom tercile +1.61% (t 6.2) in discovery and +1.59% (t 4.0) in confirmation.
  • Different people buying gaps more than one insider repeating: +1.72% vs +0.86% in discovery, and replicated (+1.07%, t 2.6).
  • Neither predicts returns after the open. They are only useful to someone who can buy before the open.

Insider information vs ordinary momentum

Insider minus matched control (mean)Issuer median of meansAverage round-trip cost (0.72%)

All discovery events (n ≈ 5,000–5,300), entry at the official open, 10 matched controls each. Days 0–1 are positive in every control design (t ≥ 2.1) and survive global multiple-testing correction. From day 2 the mean is tail-driven and the median turns negative. Everything is below the cost line.

  • Chart patterns are generic. Winners had fallen into the filing and losers had run up or broken out, but matched controls show the same reversal (5-day return spread −1.44%, t −3.9).
  • Big gaps fade, and that is generic too. The top-decile gap surprise lost −1.8% on day 0. Ordinary stocks with the same gap faded more; insider gaps fade less.
  • A strong filing with no gap does not drift up later (no underreaction).
  • Size doesn't matter after the open. Tiny buys were among the biggest winners, and both $14M+ buys lost (TORO −24.5%, PAR −24%).

After-hours: how fast does the edge disappear?

Gross capture, fill → next open (hover for the share already priced: 67% at 1 minute, ≈78% from 5 minutes)Round-trip cost at 0.25% per side

After-close filings in the cached trade sample, discovery (n 196 common events). The close → next-open move averaged +3.04%. The auditor's caveat: this is a ratio of means; per event, a median of 52–69% is already priced, and 15–20% of first prints overshoot the next open.

After-hours entries look slightly better at the 1-minute speed, but only net of an assumed 0.25% spread that was never observed. They are negative at 0.5%. They also allow only a few thousand dollars per filing.

Independent audit findings

  • No look-ahead in any deep-2 feature or outcome. The embargo held: 137,343 checks, 0 violations. The new purchase classifier is point-in-time.
  • Multiple testing. 716 deep-2 discovery tests, 172 with q < 0.10, and none is a positive net-of-cost long rule. The positive survivors are gaps and residuals; the significant long rules all lose.
  • Recomputation. Recomputed independently with the auditor's own code; F1/F2 match exactly.
  • Look-ahead found in F3's size filter, which used trades after the fill.
  • Matched controls. Excluding days before later filings makes them slightly conservative. The conclusions hold.
  • Verdicts: behaviour CONFIRMED; chart CONFIRMED; execution "no candidate" CONFIRMED; "two-thirds priced" WEAKENED; the CEO/CFO near-miss REFUTED.
Correction to the previous (deep 1) report

Deep 1 compared the insider's reported (raw) price with split-adjusted price history. For 557 events whose stock later did a reverse split, "price vs prior close" therefore encoded the future split. The deep-1 findings "purchases far below the prior close underperform" and the "+7.1% best training rule" in its decay table are contaminated, as are hypotheses H23, H24 and H26 and the price-level quintiles. Deep 1's main conclusions (no next-open edge, the opening gap, the late-filing warning) do not use these fields. The deep-1 page should be read with this in mind; I can correct and republish it if you want.

What would actually move this forward

  1. Quotes plus extended-hours and premarket trades for the 3,739 after-close and about 1,500 overnight or premarket filings. The opening gap (+1.5–3% over look-alikes, t 11–14) is the only robust signal, and it can only be reached before the open. The after-hours result flips sign between 0.25% and 0.5% cost, so real spreads decide it. This needs your approval (Alpaca Basic, no extra cost, but a new historical download).
  2. Minute bars for the 1,981 filings published during market hours, for same-session entries.
  3. Older history (5–10 years). One year gives only about 15 CEO/CFO ≥ $250k events a month, so detecting a +0.5% effect needs about 1,000 events (≈5 years forward).
  4. Zero-cost forward logging (no trades): for each new CEO/CFO personal open-market filing, record publication time, platform alert time, and the first quotes and trades. This also measures InsiderTracker's real detection delay, which decides whether after-hours entry is even possible.
