Insider clusters with Bollinger Band exits: a portfolio backtest

Clusters of two or more insiders buying (at least one an officer, $250k or more), entered at the next open after the SEC filings went public. Exits use 20-day, 2.5-standard-deviation Bollinger Bands, with 1.5% risk per trade. Rules and every open definition were fixed and hashed before anything was computed.

The full strategy lost 2.2% over 11 months while SPY gained 10.8%. It is not profitable after realistic costs.

Four variants on the same signals

Per-trade results take every signal with no capacity limit. Portfolio results start from $100,000, with at most 10 positions and 3 new ones per week, and same-day signals ranked by ΔOwn. A, B and C put 10% of equity in each position; D sizes each position so that hitting the stop loses 1.5% of equity.

Similar stocks: for every signal, 5 stocks matched on dollar volume, prior 30- and 60-day return, volatility and price, entered the same morning and closed with the same exit rules. They come only from stocks whose earlier insider-purchase filings were already public, so no future information picks them. These are still stocks with insider filings, not the whole market. Interval = 90% issuer-clustered bootstrap.

Data coverage and what is blocked

  • SEC filings: 1 Dec 2025 → 2 Nov 2026, with 24–31 Dec 2025 missing. The November 2025 backfill is not finished: it started 5 Nov 2026 and was on day 1 of 18. It was not used and not touched.
  • Prices: Alpaca daily bars, split-adjusted, 1 Oct 2025 → 3 Nov 2026, covering only the 2,428 tickers that ever had an insider purchase. Dividends are not included. Tickers with no price history are counted in the funnel. No traded stock delisted during a trade; positions still open on 3 Nov are marked at that day's close and counted separately.
  • Earnings-crash filter: blocked. No point-in-time earnings dates are cached, so the filter was not applied; a labelled proxy is in the sensitivity table.
  • SPAC / blank-check exclusion: issuer-name rule only (no SIC 6770 data cached); 5 rows removed. The IPO/offering check also uses a 424B4 prospectus list that covers only 645 issuers; 15 rows removed.
  • Costs: spread, slippage and fees by liquidity, 5 / 15 / 35 / 75 bps per side, plus 25 bps extra on stop fills. Gaps through a stop fill at the open.
  • Train/test: entries before 1 Jun 2026 are train, later ones test. Nothing was tuned. June–November prices were looked at in earlier studies for other questions, so the test period is not pristine.

Definitions fixed before running

  • Signal time: at each SEC acceptance, only filings accepted by then count. Purchases must be within 5 calendar days before that filing. Because of this rule, the 14-day window never comes into play. After a signal, the same company can't signal again for 30 days.
  • Distinct insiders: reporting CIK. Joint filers, and two people reporting the same indirect holding, count as one. An insider counts only if their average price is at least $5.
  • Officer: any C-level, President, VP, Treasurer, Secretary, General Counsel, Controller or "officer" title. Directors and 10% owners alone don't count.
  • ΔOwn: for each insider and holding line, the holding before buying = shares owned after minus shares bought (taking the lowest such value on that line). Cluster ΔOwn = total shares bought ÷ total shares held before, over lines with a positive prior holding. Lines with no prior holding are invalid and left out of ΔOwn, but still count toward insiders and value.
  • Bollinger Bands: 20-day simple moving average ± 2.5 standard deviations of closes (population SD), using completed days only. Stop = lower band on the day before entry × 0.98.
  • Order of checks each day: pending dynamic exit at the open, then stop (gap fills at the open), then take-profit at the previous day's upper band (gap fills at the open), then the dynamic-stop check on the close, then the 45-session time stop at the close. If both the stop and the take-profit are touched on the same day, the stop is assumed to come first. The fixed exit for A and B is the close of the 20th session.
  • Portfolio sizing: shares are sized from the previous close, because the opening price isn't known when the order is placed. The order is cancelled if the stock opens at or below the stop, or more than 7% above the insiders' price (D). Each position is capped at 20% of equity and by available cash, with no leverage. Entries use cash available before that day's exits.
